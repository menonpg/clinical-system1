Decision models in November 2026

TypeSafe launched Jev and the System One API on 15 September 2026. Seven weeks later, OpenAI, Microsoft, Perplexity, Cloudflare, Liquid AI, IBM, H2O.ai, Google Chrome and more than a dozen independent groups had shipped decision models, and llama.cpp served the same API natively. This page summarises what changed and what it means for clinical checklists.

What it means for this project

  • One client drives them all. Most open models speak the System One request shape, and llama.cpp serves Laya, Clef, H2O-Lightning and pplx-decider directly, so the app and the experiments needed one generic backend (SystemOneBackend) to add each new model.
  • Leaderboards are a partial guide. The strongest models we could run locally (Clef-flash, Kev-27B) were also strong on our clinical checklists, but Laya, ranked 251st of 265 on the general Decision Index and near chance zero-shot here too, became competitive after an 85-minute fine-tune on a laptop. Measure on your own task.
  • Clinical prior work now exists. Hosted Jev for trial criteria (He et al.), the institution-scale CS1-4B (Mahmood lab), and MedJev, whose finding that 50–200 labels fix patient-level calibration matches our Step 5.
  • Governance decides hosted vs local. Only OpenAI states HIPAA eligibility. Local models need no agreement at all.
  • A priority dispute. Laya's author has publicly claimed priority over Jev's architecture. We cite both and take no position.
