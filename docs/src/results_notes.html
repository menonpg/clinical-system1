Open decision models improved sharply between September and November 2026. With a deterministic hook that states the smoking arithmetic, the best zero-shot open models (Kev-27B and Cloudflare Clef-flash 9B) reached AUROC 1.000 on hand-written lung notes. A 421M Laya encoder fine-tuned for 85 minutes on a laptop is competitive at about a tenth of the latency. For every model, a threshold measured on synthetic notes overstated what is safe on hand-written ones.

Reading the table. Laya zero-shot misjudges even the plain age question (44% right), which matches its authors' warning that the English noul head can follow its label text. Kev-4B is solid on age (98%) but weak on pack-years (77%) and recency (68%). After fine-tuning on template family A, Laya is near perfect on the unseen family-B templates. The two families share phrase vocabulary, so the hand-written notes below are the honest test.

The arithmetic criteria (pack-years and years since quitting) are where every zero-shot model fails. That is what the computed-facts hook targets.

AAA needs no arithmetic: sex, age and ever-smoked are stated directly. Kev-4B and Gemma 4 solve it zero-shot, and the checklist-AND lifts Kev-0.8B from 0.74 to 0.93 AUROC.

The stricter test. These are 40 notes (13 eligible), so the confidence intervals of the top systems overlap. With the facts hook, Clef-flash reaches AUROC 1.000 (direct) and 0.997 (conjunctive), H2O-Lightning-4B 0.989 (direct), and Kev-27B 1.000 (direct) and 0.997 (conjunctive), with the highest safe-automation share (95% at ≤5% error, conjunctive). The fine-tuned Laya reaches 0.957 and 85% at about a fortieth of Kev-27B's latency. Zero-shot Kev favours the direct question, because one weak criterion (recency) pulls its product down.

Telling eligible from excluded patients for a topically matched trial is hard. The 4B–9B decision models reach AUROC 0.82–0.85 and auto-decide at most 13% of pairs at ≤5% error. Kev-27B reaches 0.90 [0.86, 0.94] on a 150-pair subset and auto-decides 32%. All of them score above the generative baseline (0.80). For most models this is triage rather than automation.

At 124 ms per note, one laptop screens about 29,000 notes an hour, and no note leaves the machine. Ollama's prompt caching keeps Gemma 4 within about 10×.

  • Write one yes/no question per criterion, with thresholds, inclusivity and units spelled out.
  • Start with a current open decision model served by llama.cpp (Kev-27B, Clef-flash 9B and H2O-Lightning-4B were strongest here, in that order of size). If throughput or CPU-only deployment matters, fine-tune Laya on synthetic notes; it is about 10× faster.
  • State the arithmetic with a deterministic hook. It was the largest gain for every model, but check that it doesn't hurt other criteria.
  • Keep the per-criterion audit, and choose between the product and the direct question on your labelled set.
  • Measure the automation threshold on your own notes. Synthetic-note thresholds overstated safe coverage by up to 2.6×.
