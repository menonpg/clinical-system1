Laya vs Kev

Both projects are open, locally runnable versions of the same idea: a model that answers typed questions about a text by prefill only, with calibrated probabilities. They reach it from opposite ends. Laya starts from a bidirectional encoder, Kev from a decoder LLM.

What that predicts for clinical checklists

  • Arithmetic and thresholds (pack-years = packs/day × years, "quit in 2012" against a 15-year window, age 50–80) need reasoning over numbers. A decoder base should do better than a 400M encoder zero-shot.
  • Throughput and footprint favour Laya: it fits on a CPU-only hospital workstation.
  • Specialisation favours Laya as well. Full fine-tuning of a 421M encoder takes minutes to an hour on a laptop GPU, while Kev's Mac training path is slow for Qwen3.5.
  • Both give a probability per criterion. That enables the two things a clinical workflow needs: an audit trail per checklist item, and selective automation, where confident cases are auto-screened and the rest go to a clinician.

The study design tests these predictions directly, and the results page reports what we found.
