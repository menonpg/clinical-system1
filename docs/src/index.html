Clinical System One

Standard-of-care checklists answered by small, local, calibrated decision models. Each criterion becomes a typed yes/no question, the model answers from one prefill pass on a laptop, and a probability per criterion decides what can be automated and what goes to a clinician.

LayaEncoder decision model (ModernBERT / mmBERT) trained with RL against proper scoring rules. KevQwen3.5 + LoRA + pointer head, speaking TypeSafe's System One API. Laya vs KevTwo routes to the same interface, and what each predicts for clinical text. Study designUSPSTF lung and AAA checklists, 40 hand-written notes, TREC Clinical Trials 2021. ResultsAccuracy, calibration, share automatable at a 5% error budget, latency. ReproduceEvery command, from clone to figures.

The idea in one request

state = "Visit 2026. 68 M, CAD s/p PCI 2019. Ex-smoker, 45 pk-yr hx, quit after his MI in 2019."
questions = {
  "lcs_age":        {"type": "noul", "instructions": "Is the patient between 50 and 80 years old, inclusive?"},
  "lcs_pack_years": {"type": "noul", "instructions": "Does the patient have a smoking history of at least 20 pack-years?"},
  "lcs_recency":    {"type": "noul", "instructions": "Does the patient currently smoke, or did they quit 15 or fewer years ago?"},
  "lcs_limiting":   {"type": "noul", "instructions": "Does the patient have a health problem that substantially limits life expectancy, ..."},
}
# one call, every answer is P(yes); eligibility = p_age * p_py * p_recency * (1 - p_limiting)
# confident -> auto-screen; uncertain -> clinician queue; every "no" names the criterion that failed
